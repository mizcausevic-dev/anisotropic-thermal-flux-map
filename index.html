Model, evidence grades, and limits what is verified, what is a model result, what is illustrative

Mechanism verified, textbook

Fourier conduction with a tensor conductivity: q = −κ∇T. When κ_r ≠ κ_θ, q is not parallel to −∇T, so flux is not normal to isotherms. In steady state with no sources, ∇·q = 0. Alternating concentric laminae of two materials give a low effective κ_r (series) and a high effective κ_θ (parallel). A graded annulus with κ_r→0 and κ_θ→∞ toward R1 (transformation design) leaves the exterior field undisturbed in the 2D steady model.

Model outputs computed, not measured

Fields are closed-form solutions of 2D steady conduction on an infinite plane with a uniform far-field gradient: core κ_c, shell with constant (κ_r, κ_θ) or the graded transformation profile, background κ0. The on-screen angle at P, the disturbance curve, and the energy residual are evaluated at runtime from these solutions. Axes are qualitative.

Illustrative parameters not from the reference

R2/R1 = 2, κ_c = 0.1 κ0, shell geometric-mean conductivity √(κ_r κ_θ) = κ0, 12 laminae, ratio sweep 1→60, comparison ratio 3. None of these describe a specific experiment or product. No dimensions, materials, temperatures, efficiencies, or certifications are stated.

Limits retained on screen segment E

  • Steady state only. Transient response not shown.
  • Core sits at a uniform intermediate temperature in the ideal design. Not a cold core.
  • Thermal conduction only. No optical invisibility.
  • The ideal graded profile is not realizable; layered builds leave residual exterior distortion.
  • Not modeled: contact resistance, finite layer count, 3D leakage, convection, radiation.
  • The plate outline is a drawing window, not a modeled boundary. Schematic, not to scale.

Safeguards built into the file
